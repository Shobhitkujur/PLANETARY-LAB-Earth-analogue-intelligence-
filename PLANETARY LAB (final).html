Data sources and assumptions
  • Published (NASA): mass 1,025 kg, wheel diameter 0.526 m, six wheels, rocker-bogie suspension, MMRTG output about 110 W at launch, top speed about 0.042 m/s. Check each against the NASA press kit before presenting.
  • Assumptions (unpublished): wheel width 0.40 m, peak torque 150 N·m per wheel, drivetrain efficiency 85%, battery 2,000 Wh, housekeeping load 50 W, thermal conductance 1.5 W/K, minimum electronics temperature 233 K, rolling coefficient 0.02, slip limit 50%.
  • Soil: the loose sand-like preset uses textbook dry-sand Bekker and Janosi values; the others are invented scenarios. None describe a real site.
  • Radiation: Moon is Chang’e-4 LND (far side, not the pole). Mars is Curiosity RAD (Gale, not Jezero). Earth values are rough background scenarios. Verify all dose rates against the papers before submission.
  • Terrain: built-in patches are real elevation (ArcticDEM for Haughton, Copernicus GLO-30 for the Ladakh box). Uploaded GeoTIFFs are cut to a 64×64 patch in your browser and never leave it. Rocks and craters are random seeded scenario features, not measurements. Rocks taller than one wheel diameter count as hard obstacles (a rule of thumb), and dense patches of them are blocked or slowed using a simple density model. Analogue similarity scores come from the separate Python package.
